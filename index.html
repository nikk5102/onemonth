A LETTER I NEEDED TO WRITE

The One Month
That Meant Years to Me.

♡

Some chapters are short. Their echoes aren't.

There's something I don't think you will ever understand.

Our relationship, or whatever it was between us, lasted barely a month.

I know how small that sounds.

One month. Thirty days, perhaps even less. Nothing compared to the years people spend building a life together. Nothing that should probably take nine months to get over, according to people who measure love by calendars.

But what nobody understands is that I wasn't falling for you for just one month.

I had liked you for years.

You were my crush long before you became someone I could call mine. I had imagined what it would feel like if, someday, you looked at me differently. And then one day, you actually proposed.

Do you have any idea what that felt like for me?

I was on cloud nine.

Something I had secretly wished for, something I had probably imagined a hundred times, was finally happening. The person I had liked for so long wanted to be with me. For once, I didn't have to imagine what it would be like. I was actually living it.

I was so happy. So excited. So emotionally invested.

I wasn't counting the days. I wasn't thinking about how long it would last. I wasn't treating it like some temporary experience.

I was simply happy that it was you.

I allowed myself to dream. I started imagining little things, conversations, memories, a future. I let myself believe that perhaps this time, something I had wanted for so long was finally going to stay.

And then, suddenly, you decided we couldn't be together anymore.

Just like that.

I was still somewhere in the beginning of my happiness, while you had already decided it was time to end things.

I don't think you will ever understand the difference between those two moments.

For you, maybe it was a month-long relationship that didn't work out.

For me, it was years of having feelings for someone, finally getting the chance to love him, and losing him just when I had started believing that I could actually have him.

I didn't even get enough time to become used to being yours, but somehow I had to learn how to live with not being yours.

And that hurts in ways I cannot properly explain.

I understood you. But who understood me?

You know what's funny?

I think I accepted the ending even before it completely happened.

I am someone who tries to understand things before they happen. I overthink every possible situation, prepare myself for disappointments, and sometimes convince myself to accept things even when they are breaking my heart.

So when you decided you didn't want to continue, a part of me immediately tried to understand your perspective.

I thought, okay. If he doesn't want to stay, I cannot force him.

If he isn't happy here, I don't want him to feel trapped.

If leaving is what he wants, then perhaps loving him means allowing him to leave.

I kept telling myself that I loved you, and if I genuinely loved you, I should want you to be happy, even if that happiness didn't include me.

And I still mean that.

I don't want someone to stay with me out of guilt, pressure or obligation. I don't want to convince someone to choose me. I don't want a love that I have to beg for.

But there's a difference between accepting someone's decision and not being hurt by it.

I understood your point of view, but that doesn't mean my heart understood what was happening.

I gave your feelings so much space that I sometimes forgot I had feelings too.

I was trying so hard to be mature about everything that I didn't give myself permission to fall apart.

And perhaps that's why I'm still carrying so much of it.

I want you to be happy. But I don't know how to imagine you with someone else.

This is the part I am most ashamed of admitting.

I want you to have a beautiful life. I want you to achieve everything you dream about. I want you to be happy, healthy and loved.

But I cannot lie and say that the thought of you with someone else doesn't hurt.

Because someday, you will probably have someone.

You'll have a wife, a home, a family, ordinary mornings, little arguments, celebrations, anniversaries and all those beautiful things people build together.

And the girl standing beside you will be someone else.

She will get to experience the things I once imagined experiencing with you.

She will hear about your day. She will know what makes you laugh. She will probably have little traditions with you. She will be there for the milestones I once secretly imagined celebrating by your side.

And I know I cannot do anything about it.

That's life, I suppose.

We don't get everything we want. Sometimes the people we imagine our entire future with become people whose future we have to watch from a distance.

But God, how do I make peace with that?

How do I accept that someone else might live the life I once pictured so clearly?

I don't want to hate the person who comes into your life. She won't have done anything wrong. She will simply be someone you choose.

And perhaps that's what will hurt the most: knowing that one day you might choose someone the way I once wished you would choose me.

I hope I never become bitter enough to wish you unhappiness.

But I also hope nobody asks me to pretend that imagining all of this doesn't break something inside me.

You left, but the part of me that loved you never really did.

Sometimes I feel like the part of me that loved you is still somewhere inside me.

She hasn't disappeared.

She still remembers the excitement of your proposal. She still remembers how happy she was. She still remembers the dreams she started building when she thought she finally had a chance with you.

But I think I have lost something along the way.

I feel like I've lost my ability to love someone again.

I don't feel excited about marriage anymore. I look at my parents and see the kind of love I would want for myself. I look at my father and think, will I ever find someone who loves me the way he loves my mother?

And even if I do, will I ever be able to give that person the kind of love he deserves?

What if I spend the rest of my life comparing people to a feeling I couldn't hold on to?

What if someone genuinely good comes into my life, but I am too emotionally exhausted to let him in?

I know this might sound dramatic. I know I'm young and life has so much left to offer.

But right now, that's honestly how I feel.

Do you remember how we used to talk about growing old together? How we joked about living like uncle and aunty and having our own little happily-ever-after?

I remember.

I remember those conversations because I attached meaning to them.

Maybe you have forgotten them. Maybe they were just cute conversations to you, things people say when they're happy and caught up in a moment.

But I remember them because I actually imagined that life.

I imagined the ordinary things. The comfortable silences. The silly arguments. The growing old. The little home. The version of us that would exist years later.

I know we were together for such a short time. I know those conversations weren't promises carved in stone.

But they meant something to me.

And now I don't even know what to do with those memories.

And then came the rumours.

As if losing you wasn't already difficult enough, I had to hear things about myself that I couldn't even recognise.

I heard that I was being used.

I heard that I was a gold digger.

I heard that I wasn't a good girl.

I heard things about my character, my intentions and the kind of person I supposedly was.

And I kept asking myself:

What?

When?

How did a situation that was already emotionally difficult become an opportunity for people to create stories about me?

I accepted that we dated. I never denied what happened between us. I never pretended that you were someone you weren't. I knew what I felt, and I was honest about it.

But suddenly, I was hearing things that made me question how people could even create such an image of me.

I wish someone had asked me first.

I wish someone had cared enough to hear my side before deciding who I was.

I wish people understood that a girl's character isn't something they can casually discuss just because they don't like her, because things didn't work out, or because their advances weren't reciprocated.

And to the people who have been spreading these stories:

Please, shut the fuck up and get a life.

Seriously.

If I rejected you, that doesn't give you the right to invent stories about me. If you couldn't get what you wanted from me, that doesn't give you permission to damage my character.

My decision to say no does not make me a bad person.

My past relationship does not give you ownership over my reputation.

And being a girl who once loved someone does not make me a gold digger, a characterless person or whatever other label you have decided to attach to my name.

You don't know my heart. You don't know my intentions. You don't know the person I am when nobody is watching.

So please stop making statements about a life you know nothing about.

I have enough emotions to deal with. I shouldn't have to defend my character against stories that were never true in the first place.

I wish people would understand that there is a real human being behind the rumours. Someone who reads those words, thinks about them, questions herself and gets hurt.

I am not a character in your little gossip story.

I am a person.

And I deserve basic respect.

Maybe I was the only one who fell this hard.

I don't know what your experience of those days was.

I don't know how much you felt, how much you remember, or whether you ever look back at that month with the same tenderness that I do.

Maybe you moved on.

Maybe you stopped thinking about it.

Maybe it was never as deep for you as it was for me.

And I cannot force you to remember something the way I remember it.

But I wish you knew that I wasn't playing around.

I wasn't passing time.

I wasn't looking for benefits.

I wasn't treating you as an option.

I was a girl who had liked you for years, who was incredibly happy when you finally chose her, and who genuinely believed that maybe something beautiful was beginning.

I didn't need a lifetime to know that my feelings were real.

And I didn't need years of dating to be hurt when it ended.

Sometimes the shortest chapters leave the longest marks.

I think that's what happened to me.

You were a month in my life, but you were years in my imagination.

And I am still trying to separate the person you actually were from the future I had imagined with you.

I don't know when that will happen.

But I hope one day it does.

They just need somewhere to exist.
